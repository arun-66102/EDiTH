# Diabetic Retinopathy — Clinical Staging & Management Guidelines

## DR Severity Classification (International Clinical DR Disease Severity Scale)

### Grade 0 — No Apparent DR
- **Findings**: No retinal abnormalities detected.
- **Follow-up**: Annual dilated fundus examination.
- **Management**: Optimize glycemic control (target HbA1c < 7.0%).

### Grade 1 — Mild Non-Proliferative DR (NPDR)
- **Findings**: Microaneurysms only.
- **Follow-up**: Annual screening. Re-examine in 12 months.
- **Management**: Optimize glycemic, blood pressure, and lipid control. Patient education on the importance of regular screening.

### Grade 2 — Moderate NPDR
- **Findings**: Microaneurysms plus any of: dot/blot hemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton wool spots (fewer than those in severe NPDR).
- **Follow-up**: Re-screen in 3–6 months. Refer to ophthalmology if macular edema is suspected.
- **Management**: Intensify systemic management. Ophthalmology referral recommended. Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT) to evaluate for diabetic macular edema (DME).

### Grade 3 — Severe NPDR
- **Findings**: Any of the following (4-2-1 rule):
  - Severe intraretinal hemorrhages in all 4 quadrants
  - Venous beading in ≥ 2 quadrants
  - Prominent intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA) in ≥ 1 quadrant
- **Follow-up**: Re-examine in 2–4 months.
- **Management**: Urgent ophthalmology referral. Consider pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP) especially if follow-up is uncertain. ~50% risk of progression to PDR within 1 year.

### Grade 4 — Proliferative DR (PDR)
- **Findings**: Neovascularization (NVD or NVE), vitreous or pre-retinal hemorrhage, fibrous proliferation, tractional retinal detachment.
- **Follow-up**: Immediate treatment. Follow-up every 2–4 weeks during active treatment.
- **Management**:
  - Pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP) — standard of care.
  - Anti-VEGF intravitreal injections (e.g., ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab) — may be used alone or with PRP.
  - Vitrectomy — indicated for non-clearing vitreous hemorrhage (> 1 month) or tractional retinal detachment involving the macula.

## Diabetic Macular Edema (DME)
- Can occur at any stage of DR.
- **Diagnosis**: OCT showing central retinal thickness > 300 μm, or clinically significant macular edema (CSME) on exam.
- **Treatment**: Anti-VEGF intravitreal injections (first-line), focal/grid laser photocoagulation, intravitreal corticosteroid implants.

## Key Lesion Definitions

| Lesion | Description |
|---|---|
| **Microaneurysm** | Small, round, dark-red dots. Earliest clinical sign of DR. Outpouchings of capillary walls. |
| **Dot/Blot Hemorrhage** | Intraretinal hemorrhages. Dot hemorrhages are small; blot hemorrhages are larger and deeper. |
| **Hard Exudate** | Yellow-white deposits of lipid and protein that have leaked from damaged capillaries. Often arranged in a circinate pattern around leaking microaneurysms. |
| **Cotton Wool Spot** | White, fluffy patches caused by ischemic infarction of the retinal nerve fiber layer. Indicate retinal ischemia. |
| **Venous Beading** | Localized dilation and constriction of retinal veins, giving a beaded appearance. Sign of retinal ischemia. |
| **IRMA** | Intraretinal microvascular abnormalities — dilated, tortuous intraretinal capillaries. Distinguished from neovascularization by their intraretinal location. |
| **Neovascularization** | Growth of new, abnormal blood vessels. NVD = at the disc; NVE = elsewhere. Hallmark of proliferative DR. |
| **Vitreous Hemorrhage** | Bleeding into the vitreous cavity from neovascular vessels. Can cause sudden vision loss. |
| **Tractional Retinal Detachment** | Fibrovascular membranes contract and pull the retina away from the underlying tissue. |

## Risk Factors for DR Progression
- Duration of diabetes (most significant factor)
- Poor glycemic control (elevated HbA1c)
- Hypertension
- Dyslipidemia
- Pregnancy
- Nephropathy / renal disease
- Smoking

## Screening Recommendations
- **Type 1 Diabetes**: Initial screening 5 years after diagnosis, then annually.
- **Type 2 Diabetes**: Screen at diagnosis, then annually.
- **Pregnancy**: Screen before conception or in the first trimester, then every trimester.
